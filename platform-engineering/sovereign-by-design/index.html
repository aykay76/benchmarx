Platform engineering

Sovereign by design.

Your tools, your cloud, your telemetry only mean something if you could actually move them. Sovereignty is an engineering property, not a marketing word.

5 min read

Four honest questions

Where does the data live?

Not just the primary store: backups, logs, caches and telemetry too.

Who can access it?

Including the operator of the service, and anyone they delegate to.

What do we depend on?

Every service, SDK and licence that would stop working if a supplier changed course.

How hard is it to leave?

Hard to answer in theory. Much easier if you've rehearsed it.

Portability comes from boundaries

The most practical route to sovereignty is to build on open, widely implemented interfaces, so that any individual component can be replaced without rewriting everything around it. Container images, the Kubernetes API, OpenTelemetry, standard SQL databases and infrastructure as code are all examples.

The aim isn't to avoid every proprietary service. It's to know where you've accepted one, why, and what it would cost to unpick.

Control has a price

More control means more responsibility. It's a trade-off, not a virtue, and the right answer differs by component.

ApproachYou gainYou pay
Fully managed SaaSSpeed, no operationsLeast control, highest dependency
Managed services in your own accountControl of data and access, less toilSome provider lock-in, configuration work
Self-hosted open sourceMost control, easiest to moveYou run it: patching, upgrades, on-call

Sovereignty isn't all or nothing. Decide component by component what you must control and what you're happy to rent, and write the reasoning down.

Where this gets hard

What surprises teams after the launch

The identity provider, the CI service, the licence server, the SDK that quietly phones home. A real dependency map nearly always turns up surprises, and it's much cheaper to draw before you need it.

An exit plan nobody has tested is a hope. Rehearsing even a partial move shows what depends on what, and how long it would genuinely take.

Self-hosting moves cost from the invoice to the team: upgrades, security patches, capacity, on-call. Make sure that cost is planned and staffed, not discovered.

Residency and sector rules differ by region and change over time. Designing for configurable boundaries is safer than hard-coding today's reading of them.
